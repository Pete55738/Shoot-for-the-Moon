    What this is

    A one-button space programme. Press LAUNCH. The rocket flies as high as its parts allow, earns money for the altitude, and comes back under a parachute. While the crew turns it around you buy better parts. Ten tiers of engine, tank, aerodynamics and hull get a rocket to the Moon; once you are there, every Moon flight delivers a module until the base is complete.

    The one lever

    When to press LAUNCH is never a choice — it is always right to fly. The decision is what to buy between launches. Payload tiers pay more per flight but only ride a rocket at least as good as they are, and no part may run more than two tiers ahead of the weakest one, so a lopsided rocket is impossible.

    How the numbers work

    The flight is a real (small) rocket equation: thrust against mass, gravity that weakens with height, drag that thins with the air. Whatever energy is left at engine cut-off decides the apogee, and enough of it is escape — that is the Moon. Nothing is random; the same rocket flies the same every time. Earnings are a gentle power of altitude times the payload multiplier, plus a one-time bonus at each milestone. Balance was measured over five buying strategies in a headless sim (DEV → Run balance sim): between 70 and 100 launches to the Moon for anyone who buys what is affordable, and no strategy waits more than 17 launches for its next purchase.
