      What this is

      A one-button space programme. Press LAUNCH. The rocket flies as high as its parts allow, earns money for the altitude, and comes back under a parachute. While the crew turns it around you buy better parts. Ten tiers of engine, tank, aerodynamics and hull get a rocket to the Moon; once you are there, every Moon flight delivers a module until the base is complete.

      The missions

      Five missions, in order: Earth low orbit, lunar-injection orbit, lunar orbit, a lunar base, and a Moon base of 10,000 people. Each needs the rocket's delivered Δv to clear the real requirement (9.3, 12.5, 13.4 and 15.3 km/s) and two pieces of equipment. When both are true, LAUNCH turns gold and the next flight is the milestone flight. Every milestone unlocks two more tiers on every track. After the base is established, every flight lands cargo and the population grows until 10,000 people live there.

      The one lever

      When to press LAUNCH is never a choice — it is always right to fly. The decision is what to buy between launches. Payload tiers pay more per flight but only ride a rocket at least as good as they are, and no part may run more than two tiers ahead of the weakest one, so a lopsided rocket is impossible.

      How the numbers work

      The flight is a real (small) rocket equation: thrust against mass, gravity that weakens with height, drag that thins with the air. Whatever energy is left at engine cut-off decides the apogee, and enough of it is escape — that is the Moon. Nothing is random; the same rocket flies the same every time. Earnings are a gentle power of altitude times the payload multiplier, plus a one-time bonus at each milestone. Balance was measured over five buying strategies in a headless sim (DEV → Run balance sim): between 70 and 100 launches to the Moon for anyone who buys what is affordable, and no strategy waits more than 17 launches for its next purchase.

      The railgun

      Once 1,000 people live on the Moon a railgun is built beside the base. It fires one cargo shipment back to Earth during every turnaround, worth about 90 cents per resident, so the colony starts paying for itself. It never replaces launching — at 10,000 people a shipment is roughly a fifth of what a cargo run earns.

      Scoring

      Distances are shown in miles and speeds in miles per hour; the sim underneath is metric and is converted only at display time.

      Records are kept on this device only. There is no shared leaderboard yet; if one arrives it follows the house rules in docs/GAMES.md.

      Start over

      Your rocket, funds and records live in this browser only — nothing is stored anywhere else, so resetting here cannot affect anyone else's game. Redraw is safe: it rebuilds the screen and keeps your progress, which is the one to reach for if the picture ever looks wrong. Reset my progress puts you back at tier 1 with no money and no records, and asks first.
